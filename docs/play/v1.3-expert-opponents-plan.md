# Play Solver v1.3 — Expert opponents

A toggle that makes the solver **infer from the opponents' plays**, not only
from which cards they played. Status: **design draft, 2026-09-01**, revised
the same day after review: opponents are judged **by what they could see**
(a recursive Monte-Carlo), not double-dummy. Renumbered from v1.2 to v1.3 the
same day — points/IMP costs go first. The open choices at the end are small.

## The gap it closes

v1.0 samples the two hidden hands consistent with the play so far in the
weakest sense: a hidden seat holds the cards it has played, and a seat that
showed out is capped in that suit. Nothing else about *how* they played
enters. So at trick 8, when East followed low to a diamond at trick 3 instead
of returning one for West to ruff, the sampler still deals East the last
diamond as often as the card counts allow — and grades declarer's play on
layouts a decent player would have ruled out.

Under **Expert opponents** a sampled layout must also satisfy: *on this
layout, every earlier play by an opponent was a best play **given what that
opponent could see at the time**.* The layout where East held the last
diamond and a ruff was available is rejected, because on it East — looking at
East's own hand, dummy and the play so far — would have found the ruff. The
surviving sample is the one a strong player reasons with, and the graded best
card moves with it: the second-order inference ("they didn't, so they can't")
that no free tool does.

## Definition — a Monte-Carlo inside the Monte-Carlo

For a graded decision at play index `i`, viewed by seat `V` with opponents
`O` (both defenders when `V` is declarer; declarer alone when `V` is a
defender — partner is *not* judged, see below):

1. **Outer sample.** Draw a layout `L` exactly as v1.0 does (pinned cards,
   show-outs, the user's per-seat constraints).
2. **Judge each earlier opponent decision.** For every index `j < i` where
   the player on play was some `o ∈ O` with more than one legal card:
   - `o`'s information at `j` is: `o`'s own hand **as dealt in `L`**, dummy
     (real, faced after the opening lead), the play `play[:j]`, and the
     auction — so the same per-seat constraints the user entered, because the
     auction was public to `o` too.
   - **Inner sample.** Draw `M` layouts of the two hands hidden from `o`,
     consistent with that information (the ordinary v1.0 sampler, viewed
     from `o`). DD-solve the position after `play[:j]` on each; average the
     tricks per legal card of `o`. That is `o`'s single-dummy valuation.
   - `o`'s actual card at `j` is **consistent** if its mean tricks is within
     `tolerance` of the best card's mean. Otherwise `L` is discarded.
3. Keep drawing outer layouts until `num_deals` consistent ones are found or
   the draw budget is spent; grade `i` on the survivors.

This is one level of recursion: the inner sample judges `o` against
opponents who are merely card-consistent, not themselves expert. Depth is a
parameter (`depth = 1`); depth 2 is the same code and multiplies the cost by
the inner count again — it exists as a knob, not a plan.

Only plays before `i` count, including opponents' cards in the current
partial trick. A tolerance is necessary, not optional: the inner mean is a
noisy estimate from `M` deals, and "best within noise" is the honest reading
of "found the best play".

### Partner is deliberately not judged

For a defender's grade, partner's cards are **signals to you** — a low card
in UDCA encouraging, a high one discouraging, count and suit preference — and
judging them as pure trick-maximising play would reject honest signalling
layouts. Partner stays pinned-but-unjudged as in v1.0. Reading the signals
themselves is a separate, far-later feature (see the roadmap's *Later* list:
*Partnership signalling*).

## Configuration — ratios, all exposed

| Setting | Default | Range | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `expert_opponents` | off | bool | the toggle |
| `num_deals` | 30 when on (UI swaps the default) | 5–100 | outer sample size `N` |
| `expert_inner_ratio` | 0.5 | 0.1–1.0 | inner sample size `M = max(5, round(ratio × N))` — the user's "half of the main Monte-Carlo" |
| `expert_tolerance` | 0.25 tricks | 0–1 | how far below the best mean an actual card may fall and still count as a best play |
| `expert_budget` | 20 | 5–50 | outer draws allowed per consistent layout wanted, before giving up |
| `expert_depth` | 1 | 1–2 | recursion depth (2 is exposed, not recommended) |

Defaults live in one place in the engine (`engine/play/expert.py`), the API
schema mirrors them, and the UI shows the toggle plus an *Advanced* disclosure
for the ratio and tolerance. Everything is in the request body, so the cache
key and the query log carry the settings for free.

## Cost, honestly

Today one graded decision costs `N` boards. Under this toggle it costs about
`N × budget_overhead × k_i × M` boards, where `k_i` is the number of earlier
opponent decisions with a choice (roughly `i / 2`, up to ~20 late in the
hand) and `M = ratio × N`. At `N = 30`, `M = 15`, mid-hand `k_i = 10`: ~4,500
boards per decision against 30 today — **two orders of magnitude**, before
memoisation. A full single-seat analysis is tens of minutes to an hour on the
2-vCPU VPS, minutes to tens of minutes on a 4-thread laptop. The user has
accepted that this mode is slow by nature and tuned by ratio, not avoided.
What keeps it tractable:

- **Memoisation — the load-bearing optimisation.** The inner judgement at
  `j` depends only on `(j, o, o's unplayed cards at j)`: dummy, `play[:j]`
  and the constraints are the same for every outer layout. Key the verdict
  (consistent / not, plus the two means) on exactly that. Late in the hand
  `o` holds few cards, the possible holdings are few, and the hit rate
  across outer layouts — and across the graded decisions `i` of the same
  seat, which share every `j` — is high. Early `j` (13-card holdings) rarely
  repeats, but early `j` is also where `k_i` is small. The memo lives for one
  `grade_play` call.
- **Most-recent-first, early exit.** Judge `j` in descending order; stop at
  the first inconsistency. Recent decisions prune most (the opponent had the
  most information), so rejected layouts pay for one or two inner samples,
  not `k_i`.
- **Batching.** All inner boards for one `(j, o-holding)` are one
  `dds_runtime.solve_all` batch; several pending judgements are packed into
  one ≤ 200-board batch where possible. One DDS entry per batch.
- **Progressive requests.** `AnalyzeRequest` gains an optional
  `decisions: list[int]` (play indices to grade); the frontend chunks a
  seat's analysis so the table fills in trick by trick instead of after one
  long wait. Each chunk is separately cached. (Helps v1.1 too; do it first.)
  The memo is per request, so chunking trades some memo hits for feedback —
  chunk by trick, not by decision.
- **The draw budget.** Stop after `budget × N` outer draws. Survivors below a
  floor of 8 still grade, but the response says so.

## What the response says

Every graded decision carries `sampled` (outer draws), `consistent`
(layouts graded) and `judged` (inner judgements run, memo hits excluded) when
the toggle is on; the UI shows "graded on 27 of 30 layouts consistent with
expert play". If **zero** layouts survive the budget, the decision is graded
on the unfiltered sample and marked `expert_inference: 'none'` with the note
"no sampled layout is consistent with the opponents' earlier plays — they
may have erred". That is information, not a failure: it says an opponent made
a real mistake earlier, which is worth surfacing in its own right (and with
v1.1's whole-table view, that mistake is probably already in the list).

## What is still approximate

- **Depth 1.** The opponent is modelled as a good single-dummy player whose
  own opponents are merely consistent. Real experts also read *your* plays.
  Deeper recursion is the same code at prohibitive cost.
- **Noise.** `M` deals give a noisy valuation; `tolerance` absorbs it. Too
  tight rejects honest guesses, too loose rejects nothing — the default 0.25
  is a starting point to tune against real hands.
- **Uniform prior.** Consistent layouts are kept with weight 1; a layout on
  which the opponent's play was *barely* best counts the same as one where it
  was clearly best. Soft weighting by margin is a later refinement.
- **Signals ignored.** Partner's spot cards carry information the model does
  not read (see above).

## Engine changes

- New `engine/play/expert.py`: the settings dataclass with defaults; the
  opponent-decision index (`(j, o, actual_card)` for every `j` with a
  choice, computed **once per graded seat** and sliced per `i`); the inner
  judgement (`judge(j, o, holding) → verdict`, memoised); the outer
  draw-judge-keep loop with budget and floor.
- `engine/play/grader.py`: `_sample_layouts(..., expert=None)` delegates to
  the expert loop when settings are given; `grade_play` / `grade_position`
  accept `expert` and add `sampled` / `consistent` / `judged` per decision.
  The inner sample reuses `infer_constraints` + `generate_layouts` viewed from
  `o` — the same functions, a different view seat; that reuse is the point of
  the view-parameterised design.
- **Perspective trap, twice.** `solve_board` returns tricks for the side on
  play. In the inner judgement the player on play at `j` *is* `o`, so `o`'s
  tricks are read directly; but `o`'s "best" is best **for `o`'s side**,
  which is the opposite side from the graded seat. Keep the two frames in
  separate functions and test each.
- `dds_runtime` is untouched; its batches are already ≤ 200 boards.

## API / frontend

- `PlayStateRequest`: `expert_opponents: bool = False`,
  `expert_inner_ratio`, `expert_tolerance`, `expert_budget`, `expert_depth`
  with the bounds above; validated only when the toggle is on.
- Toggle in the analysis controls with the one-sentence explanation
  (*"Assumes the opponents found the best play at every earlier turn, judged
  by what they could see — much slower"*), the deal-count default swap, and
  an *Advanced* disclosure for the ratios. Decision rows show the
  consistency count.

## Tests that carry the feature

- **Constructed end-position, brute force.** A small position (e.g. 4 cards
  each) where an opponent's earlier play is single-dummy best only on
  layouts where they lack a given card: assert every surviving layout lacks
  it, and the graded best card changes between the toggle off and on. Use
  `expert_inner_ratio = 1.0` and a large `N` so the inner mean is exact
  enough for a sharp assertion.
- **The diamond-ruff shape** from the user's example as a full-deal fixture:
  after East fails to return a diamond, declarer graded with the toggle on
  should see East's remaining-diamond frequency collapse.
- **Memo correctness**: the judgement for a repeated `(j, o, holding)` is
  returned from the memo (count `judged`), and equals a fresh evaluation
  with the same seed.
- **Budget exhaustion** returns the unfiltered grade with the marker.
- **Both frames**: a position where `o` is a defender and one where `o` is
  declarer, each judged correctly (the perspective test).
- **Tolerance**: a play exactly `tolerance` below best is consistent, one
  just beyond is not.
- API: the settings round-trip, are ignored when the toggle is off, and
  change the cache key when on.

## Open choices (small)

1. **Tolerance default** — 0.25 tricks proposed; tune on real hands.
2. **Zero-survivor fallback** — unfiltered grade with a marker (proposed) or
   the survivors of the most recent `j` only?
3. **A fast "all hands open" variant** — judging `o` double-dummy on `L`
   itself is the degenerate case `M = 1` with `L` as the inner layout. It is
   a much stricter filter (an opponent who could not see partner's hand is
   judged as if they could) and is *not* what this feature means; it could be
   exposed later as a cheap preview mode if the recursive one proves too slow
   to use interactively.
