# Play v1.4 — sample size: a confidence on every status, and more deals where the grade is in doubt

The first accuracy item folded into v1.4 (see `ROADMAP.md`, focus area 2,
and the *What is next* section of `v1.4-performance-plan.md`). The timing
work found that at 100 deals a big-swing decision's *status* flips with the
seed alone. This plan measures why, and designs two non-exclusive answers the
user asked for (2026-09-08):

- **A.** grade a decision on more deals when its first grade crosses a
  threshold — it does not read clearly optimal, or its band straddles a
  status line;
- **B.** grade a decision on more deals when the cheap *plain* scan (no
  expert filter) already flags it — the unfiltered sample as an independent
  second look, so the expert grade's own draw cannot hide a mistake.

Both sit on one free primitive: a **standard error for every graded
decision**, computed from numbers the grader already has.

## Measured facts (2026-09-08, dev laptop, 16 DDS threads)

All numbers are c7 South's trick-9 ♦6 (play index 34, the closed-room
headline error, `docs/play/board7.lin`), graded through the engine directly
with a cold memo per run (`scripts/measure_noise.py seeds`). `diff` is the mean over the
sample of the per-deal paired difference *played − best*; `se` is that
sample's own standard error, `sd(d)/√n`.

| mode | deals | seeds | diff per seed | se per seed | seed-to-seed sd | cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| plain | 100 | 0–9 | −0.49, −0.16, −0.11, −0.17, −0.31, −0.21, −0.28, −0.20, −0.18, −0.20 | 0.11–0.13 | **0.10** (mean −0.23) | 0.05 s |
| plain | 400 | 0–9 | −0.36, −0.21, −0.15, −0.14, −0.23, −0.24, −0.26, −0.25, −0.20, −0.26 | 0.06 | **0.06** (mean −0.23) | 0.1 s |
| strict | 100 | 0–4 | −0.43, −0.07, −0.03, −0.03, −0.33 | 0.13–0.14 | **0.17** (mean −0.18) | 14–15 s |
| strict | 200 | 0–1 | −0.44, −0.20 | 0.09–0.10 | — (mean −0.32) | 21 s |

IMPs, same runs: at 100 deals the decision reads −1.4 to −3.8 IMPs with a
per-run se of **±0.9 IMPs**; at 200, ±0.7. The 2-IMP demotion line sits
inside the band either way.

1. **The per-deal standard error predicts the seed spread.** Plain: se
   0.12 against a measured sd 0.10 at 100 deals, 0.06 against 0.06 at 400.
   The noise is ordinary sampling noise and the grader can quote it at no
   DDS cost, because `_solve` already returns each card's trick count on
   every board (`per_board`), index-aligned across cards.
2. **Big-swing decisions have the widest per-deal spread**, which is why
   they are the noisy ones. ♦6 is worse than ♦A on 55–63 % of layouts and
   *better* on 22–40 % (it is a genuine guess, not a plain error), so the
   paired difference ranges over ±1–2 tricks and its sd is ≈ 1.2 tricks —
   three times a 0/−1 coin's. A decision whose played card equals the best
   card on every deal has se = 0 and is firm for free.
3. **The truth is −0.23 tricks, and no sample size makes that status
   firm.** It sits between the −0.1 and −0.3 lines; 100 deals is a coin
   toss between *good* and *suboptimal*, 400 deals still leaves a 95 % band
   of ±0.12, and pinning it to one side of −0.3 at 2σ would take ~1 200
   deals. The honest output is the band, not a bigger sample alone. More
   deals shrink the band on the decisions where it matters; the band tells
   the reader when the badge is a judgement call.
4. **Strict adds filter noise on top, and raising the deal count today
   changes the filter itself.** Strict's seed sd (0.17) exceeds its se
   (0.13): which borderline layouts survive depends on the draw. Worse, the
   inner cap is `inner_ratio × num_deals`, so grading at 200 deals judges
   opponents on 100 inner deals instead of 50, the rejection threshold
   tightens from 0.27 to 0.22 tricks, the rejection rate rises from 23 % to
   41 %, and the mean moves from −0.18 to −0.32. A bigger outer sample must
   **not** redefine the verdict — the inner cap has to be decoupled from
   the escalated count (design point 4).
5. **Escalation is far cheaper than quadratic at this position.** Strict
   100 → 200 deals cost 14 → 21 s (1.45×, judgements 814 → 1042): late
   holdings repeat, so most extra layouts are memo hits, and the inner test
   stops early. Early-trick decisions, whose holdings are all distinct, will
   scale closer to linearly with the layouts drawn. (One seed-2 run took
   221 s for the same work — the laptop's DDS-rate stall the perf plan
   warns about; compare board counts, not wall clock.)

Four of the 22 are *optimal* at the point estimate but marginal — the
false-negative candidates a fixed-threshold trigger would miss (o7 W's T8

tricks, o7 W's lead on IMPs); only o7 S's T7 ♠3 (−0.37 ± 0.06, −3.98 ±
0.56 IMPs) is firm — the one call the seed can never flip.

## Design

### 1. A standard error on every graded decision (free)

`_options` / `grade_play` compute, from `per_board`, the paired per-deal
differences between the card played and the trick-best card, in tricks and
in IMPs (the IMP conversion per deal is what `imps` already does), and
report per decision:

```
sample: {
  deals: int,          # layouts the grade rests on (after any escalation)
  se_tricks: float,    # sd(played − best per deal) / √deals
  se_imps: float,
  firm: bool,          # the 2σ band lands on the point estimate's status
  escalated: bool,     # a second, larger sample was taken (2–3 below)
}
```

`firm` is decided by re-classifying the corners of the band — `diff ± 2·se`
crossed with `imp_diff ± 2·se_imps` through `classify_with_imps` — and
requiring every corner to agree with the point status. It is a **flag beside
the badge, not a fourth status**: the badge keeps its meaning and its
thresholds (v1.0/v1.2 grades stay comparable), and the flag says "this one
is a judgement call at this sample size". Per-seat summaries count marginal
decisions; the whole-table swings list carries the band.

Known bias, noted not fixed: the best card is the arg-max of sampled means,
so when two candidates are close its mean is selected upward and `diff` is
biased low by about one se ("winner's curse"). The 0.1-trick optimal band
absorbs most of it, and escalation shrinks it with the se.

### 2. Trigger A — escalate when the grade is not clearly optimal

After the base grade at `num_deals` (N), a decision is **escalated** when

- its status is not `optimal` (diff < −0.1 tricks or IMP demotion), **or**
- it is not `firm`.

Escalation grades the same decision on `factor × N` deals (default ×4:
halves the se; capped by `max_deals`) and the escalated grade replaces the
base one — status, diff, options, `sample` all from the larger sample.
Deterministic, and the base draw is untouched: a plain request grades a
whole seat from **one** rng, so if the extra layouts continued that stream
every later decision of the seat would see a different sample. The
extension therefore draws from its own stream, seeded per decision
(`random.Random((seed, play index))`), so un-escalated decisions — and the
base pass of escalated ones — are bit-identical to the current benchmarks,
and escalated grades are reproducible. Nothing is redrawn — the base
layouts are a uniform sample of the same population, so the escalated
sample is the base plus `(factor − 1) × N` more.

Cost, plain mode: 18 % of decisions × 3 extra samples ≈ +55 % boards for a
seat — from ~3.5 s to ~5.5 s on a 20-decision declarer. Plain stays one
request per seat. Expert mode: the same 18 % of decisions at 1.5–4× their
cost each (fact 5); on c7 South strict (122 s) the four escalations cost
between +20 s and +65 s. Bounded, and spent exactly on the decisions the
user reads.

### 3. Trigger B — escalate when the plain scan flags it (expert mode) — DROPPED

**Built, benchmarked and dropped the same day (ruling 2026-09-08, see
*Built* below):** on the three strict benchmarks every extension this
trigger caused ended firm optimal at 300 deals, and together they were
about 70 % of the added time. The design as it was:

In expert mode, before the expert grade of a decision, grade it plain at N
(0.05–0.2 s, under 3 % of the expert cost) and treat the plain grade's
trigger — not optimal, or not firm — as a second reason to escalate the
expert grade. The plain sample is independent of the expert draw and
unfiltered, so a decision the expert sample happened to read as a clean
optimal (a lucky draw, or a filter that rejected the layouts where the play
loses) still gets the larger sample. Union of the two triggers; the plain
numbers themselves are kept out of the response for now (a later "unfiltered
−0.49 / expert −0.28" comparison is a UI question, not an engine one).

B also covers the `inference: none` case for free: when nothing survives the
filter the expert grade *is* the plain grade, and its trigger applies.

### 4. Decouple the inner cap from the escalated deal count

`ExpertSettings.inner_cap` stays a function of the **request's** `num_deals`,
never of the escalated count: the memo context (`ctx` carries the inner cap)
is unchanged, so every verdict from the base pass is a hit, and the filter's
population is the same, so the extension is unbiased (fact 4). The threshold
shown in the UI ("rejects plays shown at least 0.27 tricks worse at 100
deals") remains true after escalation.

### 5. Extending the expert sample, and the pools

`expert_layouts` grows a `start=` argument: layouts already accepted for this
position (the base pass's), added without re-examination; the outer loop then
draws only the deficit up to the escalated `n`. `pool_put` stores the
escalated pool, and `_carry_forward` in the next decision caps the carried
pool at that decision's own `n` (a uniform pool's prefix is a uniform
sample) **before** `examine` — today `examine` judges the whole carried pool
and truncates after, which with a 400-layout pool carried into a 100-deal
decision would quadruple the judgements for nothing. That cap is a small
independent fix worth its own commit.

### 6. API, UI, benchmarks

- Request: `escalation: {factor: 4, max_deals: 800} | null` on
  `PlayStateRequest`; `num_deals` keeps its 5–200 range as the base;
  `max_deals` 100–1000. Default **on** at ×4 for both modes (ruling below);
  part of the cache key.
- Response: `Decision.sample` as above; `AnalyzeSummary.marginal` count.
  `PositionResponse` gets `sample` too (its options carry no "played card",
  so `se` there is the best-vs-runner-up band; escalation does not apply).
- UI: `−0.24 ± 0.13` in the tricks and IMPs columns (mouse-over gives the
  deal count and "escalated"), a muted badge with a `?` for a marginal
  status, "2 of 20 marginal" in the seat summary and the pair card, and a
  sidebar checkbox *Spend more deals on doubtful decisions (×4)* under the
  deals field with the cost hint updated. The expert panel's threshold line
  is unchanged (point 4).
- `bench_play.py` prints `±se` and the deals used per decision;
  `compare_bench.py`'s gate uses the reported se — statuses must match and
  `diff` must agree within `2·√(se₁² + se₂²)` — instead of the fixed ±0.05
  band, which the noise finding showed was tighter than the estimator.
  Benchmarks re-baselined once with escalation on; un-escalated decisions
  must match the current files exactly (the gate proves the base draw is
  untouched).
- `checks/play.md` `## v1.4`: the built-in example's marginal count and one
  escalated decision's deals pinned at 4 threads; releases.ts v1.4 entry
  gains an *improved* line in plain words (a ± on every grade, more deals
  spent where a grade is in doubt).

### 7. Tests

- `se` from a hand-built `per_board` (a 0/−1 coin gives √(p(1−p)/n); a
  played-equals-best board set gives 0); `firm` corner cases on both axes,
  including IMP-only marginality with a firm trick diff.
- Escalation: a stub `_solve` counting boards proves an optimal-and-firm
  decision solves N boards and a flagged one N + 3N, that the flagged
  decision's first N layouts equal the un-escalated run's, and that the
  decisions *after* an escalated one draw exactly what they draw without it
  (the per-decision extension stream).
- Expert: `start=` layouts are not re-examined (judged count unchanged),
  the inner cap and memo context equal the base pass's, the carried pool is
  capped before examination.
- Trigger B: with a stub expert grade that reads optimal and a plain grade
  that does not, the decision escalates.
- API: schema bounds, cache key includes escalation, `sample` present on
  every non-forced decision; vitest for the ± formatting and the marginal
  summary count.

## Rulings to make before building

1. **Factor and cap.** ×4 (se halves) with `max_deals` 800 plain / 400
   expert, or ×3 / 600. Recommend ×4: a second escalation round (×16) is not
   planned — the band reports what ×4 could not settle.
2. **Default on**, both modes? Recommend yes: plain is +2 s a seat, expert
   spends its extra minutes only on the decisions the badge is about.
3. **Trigger set** = not optimal ∪ not firm ∪ plain-flagged (B). Recommend
   all three; B is the only one that reaches a lucky expert draw. *Ruled
   all three, then B dropped on the benchmark numbers the same day.*
4. **Band width** 2σ, and *marginal* as a flag beside the badge rather than
   a third status. Recommend the flag: the status thresholds are what v1.0
   users learnt, and the flag is what the seed finding actually says.
5. **Inner cap decoupled** from the escalated count (fact 4). Recommend yes;
   the alternative — letting escalation also sharpen the verdicts — is a
   different feature (a bigger `inner_ratio`) the user can already ask for.

## Built (2026-09-08)

Rulings taken the same day: **×3** (caps 600 plain / 300 expert), **on by
default in both modes**, all three triggers, the 2σ band as a flag beside
the badge, the inner cap decoupled — and then, on the strict benchmark
numbers below, **trigger B dropped** (ruling later the same day: "took too
much time, drop it completely, keep the rest"). Everything else in
*Design* shipped as written, plus one thing the design did not name:
`sample.trigger` records *why* a decision was extended (`status` or
`band`), because the expert-mode cost turned out to depend on which
trigger fired — it is what made the case against B measurable.

- Engine: `grader.EscalationSettings`, `is_firm`, `_sample_stats`,
  `_assess`, `_doubt_reason`, `_stream_rng`; `_grade` returns
  `(options, per_board, deals, expert_stats, trigger)` and extends in
  place; `expert_layouts(start=, stats=, base_deals=)`; the carried pool is
  capped at `n` before examination. 16 tests in `tests/test_play_sample.py`
  (band corners, the coin's se, extension only when in doubt, the base
  draw and every later decision bit-identical with escalation on, the
  expert extension unexamined at the base inner cap, the carried-pool cap,
  the trigger consulted once per decision on the expert grade alone).
- API: `escalation` on `PlayStateRequest` (default on, `null` off, in the
  cache key), `Decision.sample`, `summary.marginal`, `PositionResponse
  .sample`, `AnalyzeResponse.escalation`.
- UI: the checkbox under the deal slider, `± se` beside the IMPs, the
  band in the *Best* cell's tooltip, the dashed `?` badge with the reason
  in its tooltip, `N marginal` and `(×3 when in doubt)` on the seat and
  pair lines and in the swings list. Verified in the real UI (Playwright)
  on Board 17 plain, whole table and expert, and Board 6.
- Tooling: `bench_play.py` writes `diff ± se`, `IMPs ± se` and a deals
  column marked `*` / `†` by trigger; `compare_bench.py` gates on
  `2·√(se₁² + se₂²)` when both runs carry a sample block;
  `scripts/pin_example.py` replays the checks' example pins through a
  local API; `scripts/measure_noise.py` holds the measurements above.

**What the benchmarks say.** Plain: the two board-7 rooms cost the same
wall time as before (~6–7 s a room through the API); of 41 graded declarer
and defender decisions per room 8–12 escalated, every status change the
gate lists is on an escalated decision and moves toward the 400-deal
truth (c7 S's T9 ♦6: −0.49 → −0.26 ± 0.07, still marginal, as a −0.23
truth must be), and every un-escalated decision is bit-identical.
Board 17's example pins moved the same way (W 0.45 · 0.82 → 0.44 · 0.69
IMPs, four of fifteen decisions extended to 120 deals, two still marginal
under expert opponents).

Strict expert mode is where the cost lives, and it is larger than the
plan's estimate (16 threads, sequential, fresh API). **These runs were
made with trigger B still in, and they are the committed
`docs/play/bench/*-strict100` files** — the user chose not to re-run the
benchmarks after dropping B (2026-09-08), so the baselines carry the `‡`
rows at 300 deals. Against the current code those decisions read their
100-deal base grade (same status — every one was firm optimal both ways —
different `diff`/`imps` in the second decimal and `deals: 100`), and the
seat times should drop by roughly the `‡` column of the table. **Next
strict run: re-baseline** (`bench_play.py --strict` on o7, c7, c14, then
commit the files), and expect `compare_bench.py` to list only the former
`‡` rows until then.

| board | before | with escalation | extensions (`*` status / `†` band / `‡` unfiltered) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| o7 | 535 s | **936 s (1.75×)** | 11: N 5 (2 ‡), E 5 (2 ‡), W 1 — the four `‡` ended firm optimal, 160 s |
| c7 | 814 s | **1 013 s (1.25×)** | 7: S 3, E 1 (‡), W 3 (2 ‡) — the three `‡` ended firm optimal, 340 s |

Declarer extensions cost 10–55 s each, defender extensions up to 175 s
(a defender's expert decision draws 1 000–3 800 layouts to keep 300).
Every `‡` extension — trigger B alone, the unfiltered grade disagreeing
with a firm-optimal expert grade — ended firm optimal at 300 deals: nine
extensions and 645 s across the three boards spent confirming a grade,
about 70 % of the added time. The status/band extensions were the ones
the band was right to doubt: o7 N's T2 ♦5 now reads −0.30 ± 0.04, exactly
on the line; c7 S's T9 ♦6 −0.16 ± 0.08 tricks but −2.03 IMPs, suboptimal
by IMPs and marginal; b14 N's T9 ♥K −0.37 ± 0.04, firm. Status changes
the gate lists (o7 N T2 ♦5 good → suboptimal, o7 N T5 ♣T good → optimal,
c7 S T2 ♣2 good → optimal, b14 W T5 ♦3 good → optimal) are all on
extended decisions within a few hundredths of a line and marked marginal;
un-escalated decisions were bit-identical in expert mode too. The knob if
the expert cost is too high is trigger B: drop it, or fire it only on a
*status* disagreement rather than a band one — that removes most of the
added time and none of the extensions the band asked for.

## Non-goals

- No change to the status thresholds or the IMP demotion lines.
- No strategy-fusion work here (the second accuracy item, after this).
- No second escalation round, no per-decision manual re-grade button (the
  band makes the case for one; v1.5's play-from-here is the natural home).
