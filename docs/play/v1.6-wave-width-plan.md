# Play v1.6 — Fewer trips to the cloud, and seats side by side

*Drafted and built 2026-10-01. Status: engine lever and frontend lever
built, tests green (454 backend, 142 frontend); prod bench pending the
deploy — see *Bench* below for what is pinned once it has run.*

The two levers [v1.5](v1.5-lambda-strict-plan.md) left KIV after its
sequential bench: the defender seats of board 7 ran 1.2–1.5× slower
through Lambda than on a 16-thread laptop, diagnosed as narrow waves. The
hand-off asked for one thing before building either: **measure rounds per
decision to confirm the diagnosis.** This doc is that measurement and what
followed from it.

## The measurement

`scripts/measure_waves.py` runs the engine in-process through a counting
`JudgeBackend` and a wrapped `_sample_layouts`, so per decision it reports
the outer **rounds** (draws of fresh layouts), the judge **waves** (one
backend `judge` call each — through Lambda, one trip of the invoke pool),
the items per wave, and how many waves carried four judgements or fewer.
The structure is what the remote path sees: both run the same `_resolve`
loop. Board 7 `o7` strict at 100 deals, escalation off, 4 DDS threads (the
prod inner schedule); the raw tables are
`bench/board7-o7-strict100-waves-{E,W}.txt`.

| Seat | Decisions judged | Rounds | Waves | Judgements | Mean wave | Waves ≤ 4 wide |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E (defender) | 7 | **207** | **611** | 5 746 | 9.4 | 199 (33 %) |
| W (defender) | 11 | **253** | **1 112** | 7 707 | 6.9 | 718 (65 %) |

The worst decisions are the ones with the lowest acceptance rate — late in
the hand, when most sampled layouts fail the expert test on some earlier
play:

| Decision | Sampled → kept | Rounds | Waves | Judgements | Waves ≤ 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E T3 ♦Q | 908 → 100 | 33 | 187 | 2 153 | 92 |
| E T5 ♣K | 1 665 → 100 | 55 | 343 | 2 720 | 216 |
| W T6 ♥5 | 1 009 → 100 | 38 | 258 | 2 067 | 146 |
| W T7 ♠7 | 1 404 → 100 | 74 | 434 | 2 261 | 320 |
| W T8 ♠2 | 886 → 100 | 40 | 174 | 713 | 132 |

So the diagnosis holds, with one refinement. The outer loop drew only the
deficit (`max(n − accepted, 5)`), so once the first round had filled most
of the quota the rest of the decision was dozens of five-layout rounds.
Each round is a *chain* of waves — one suspect per surviving layout per
wave, latest-first, so a rejection costs one judgement — and a five-layout
round's chain is two or three judgements wide. Through Lambda every wave
is an invoke round trip (~0.33 s from the VPS), paid per wave whatever
its width: W's trick-7 decision alone made 434 trips for 2 261
judgements. The remote compute per judgement is ~0.1 s and fully
parallel, so the trips, not the judging, were the wall clock.

## Lever 1 — wave width (engine)

`expert.round_size(deficit, accepted, drawn, width)` decides what the next
round draws:

- the deficit, when the backend has no fan-out to fill (`width ≤ 1`) or no
  round has measured the acceptance rate yet (`drawn == 0`);
- otherwise `ceil(deficit / rate)` with `rate = (accepted + 1) / (drawn + 2)`
  (an even prior over the fresh draws of this decision), clamped to
  `[deficit, max(deficit, width)]`.

`LocalBackend.width = 1`, so the local path is byte-identical to v1.5.
`LambdaBackend.width = group_size × concurrency` (4 × 64 = 256): one
trip of the pool. A 256-layout round carries its first wave to the pool
in one trip where v1.5 took fifty rounds to examine the same layouts.

**Why no grade moves.** `_sample_layouts` draws sequentially from the
decision's own `random.Random` (a `LayoutStream` over the shared rng), so
the layouts come out in the same order however they are batched; a
layout's verdict is a pure function of its memo key; and `examine` takes
the first `n` consistent layouts in draw order. The accepted set — and
with it the final solve, the grade, `sampled`, `consistent` and the pool
the next decision carries — is identical. What moves is cost: the
over-drawn layouts past the `n`-th accept are traced and judged too, so
`traced` and `judged` rise (the estimate aims at zero overshoot on
average; the spread is sampling noise). `tests/test_play_wire.py` pins a
wide backend bit-identical to a narrow one on both expert fixtures and in
fewer rounds under strict; `stable()` now strips `traced` with the other
cost counters.

**Predicted structure (same script, `--width 256`, local DDS so the wall
clock is compute-bound and does not move; raw tables
`bench/board7-o7-strict100-waves-{E,W}-width256.txt`):**

| Seat | Rounds | Waves | Judgements | Mean wave | Waves ≤ 4 | Grades / sampled / kept |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E, width 1 → 256 | 207 → **43** | 611 → **150** | 5 746 → 5 799 (+1 %) | 9.4 → 38.7 | 199 → 31 | identical |
| W, width 1 → 256 | 253 → **42** | 1 112 → **261** | 7 707 → 7 859 (+2 %) | 6.9 → 30.1 | 718 → 64 | identical |

Four times fewer trips for one or two per cent more judgements, every
status, `sampled` and `consistent` the same — the over-draw's waste is
the estimation noise it was designed to be. Through Lambda the trips
were the wall clock, so the defender seats should come down by roughly
that factor on the trip-bound part; the prod bench (below) is the
measurement.

## Lever 2 — seats side by side (frontend)

v1.5's decision 5 was "2–4 decisions in flight from the frontend,
accepting within-noise grade movement". Built in its **deterministic**
form: `runAnalysis` runs the seats of a pair or table concurrently
(`Promise.all` over the per-seat loop), while each seat's decisions still
go one request at a time. The reason decisions of one seat stay
sequential is the carried pool: a decision starts from the layouts its
predecessor accepted (`VerdictMemo.pool_before` — the latest pool of that
seat below its index). Two decisions of one seat in flight would make
that lookup timing-dependent — j+1 would carry from j or from j−1
depending on which request finished first — and the grade with it. Seats
have independent chains, so a pair takes about as long as its slower seat
and a table as long as its slowest, with every grade exactly the
sequential run's.

The server needed nothing: the memo is locked, DDS sits behind its
process-global lock (the local parts of concurrent requests queue there),
the result cache dogpile-protects identical requests, and the Lambda
client's pool is shared (64 workers across the seats — with the wider
waves a seat can fill it alone, so three seats queue on it; raising
`BRIDGE_WORKER_CONCURRENCY` is the knob if the bench says so).

### KIV — decisions in flight within a seat, deterministically

If a single seat still needs to be faster, the design that keeps grades
repeatable: the frontend sends decisions in **chunks** of `k` (the API
already takes a `decisions` subset), the server grades a chunk's decisions
concurrently, and every decision of the chunk carries from the pool of the
decision *before the chunk* (`pool_before(key, chunk_start)`), not from a
chunk-mate. The pool a decision starts from is then fixed by the chunking,
which the request states, so the result cache stays consistent and a
re-run reads the same grade. The cost is real and unmeasured: carrying
from `k` decisions back means `k` decisions' worth of opponent plays to
judge per layout and more carried layouts failing the consistency filter.
Measure that before building — `measure_waves.py --decisions` can grade a
decision with its predecessors skipped to price the longer carry.
